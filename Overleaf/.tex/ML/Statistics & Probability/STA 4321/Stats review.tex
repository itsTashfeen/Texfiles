% Similar to Q1
\item Resting heart rates for healthy adults are approximately normally distributed with a mean of $70$ beats per minute and a standard deviation of $5$ beats per minute. What fraction of adults have heart rates in the following intervals?
\begin{enumerate}
    \item 65 to 75 beats per minute
    \item 60 to 80 beats per minute
    \item 65 to 80 beats per minute
    \item Less than 55 or more than 85 beats per minute
\end{enumerate}

\item The scores on a standardized test are approximately normally distributed with a mean of $100$ and a standard deviation of $15$. What fraction of test-takers have scores in the following intervals?
\begin{enumerate}
    \item 85 to 115 
    \item 70 to 130 
    \item 85 to 130 
    \item Less than 55 or more than 145 
\end{enumerate}

\item The average commuting time for employees in a city is approximately normally distributed with a mean of $45$ minutes and a standard deviation of $8$ minutes. What fraction of employees commute in the following intervals?
\begin{enumerate}
    \item 37 to 53 minutes
    \item 29 to 61 minutes
    \item 37 to 61 minutes
    \item Less than 21 or more than 69 minutes
\end{enumerate}

\item The mean duration of a customer service call is $300$ seconds with a standard deviation of $40$ seconds. Assume that the durations are approximately normally distributed.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item What percentage of calls last longer than 340 seconds?
    \item What percentage of the calls last between 220 and 380 seconds?
    \item Would you expect a call to last longer than 8 minutes (480 seconds)? Why or why not?
\end{enumerate}

\item The mean weight of a breed of dog is $50$ pounds with a standard deviation of $6$ pounds. Assume the weights are approximately normally distributed.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item What percentage of these dogs weigh more than 56 pounds?
    \item What percentage weigh between 38 and 62 pounds?
    \item Would you expect to find a dog of this breed weighing more than 70 pounds? Why or why not?
\end{enumerate}

\item The mean lifespan of a specific lightbulb is $800$ hours with a standard deviation of $50$ hours. Assume the lifespans are approximately normally distributed.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item What percentage of bulbs last longer than 850 hours?
    \item What percentage last between 700 and 900 hours?
    \item Would you expect a bulb to last longer than 1000 hours? Why or why not?
\end{enumerate}

% Similar to Q3/Q4 (Properties of summations)
\item Using the properties of summations, prove that $\sum_{i=1}^{n} (y_i - c) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} y_i - nc$, where $c$ is a constant.

\item Using the properties of summations, show that $\sum_{i=1}^{n} (ay_i + b) = a\sum_{i=1}^{n} y_i + nb$, where $a$ and $b$ are constants.

\item Show algebraically that $\sum_{i=1}^{n} (y_i - \bar{y})^2 = \sum_{i=1}^{n} y_i(y_i - \bar{y})$. 

\item Prove that $\sum_{i=1}^{n} (x_i - \bar{x}) = 0$, where $\bar{x} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i$.

\item Show that $\sum_{i=1}^{n} (y_i - \mu) = n(\bar{y} - \mu)$, where $\bar{y}$ is the sample mean and $\mu$ is a constant.

\item Show that $\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} (y_i - \bar{y}) = 0$.

\begin{enumerate}

\end{enumerate}

% Answers 1-3
\item 
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Note $65 = 70 - 5$ and $75 = 70 + 5$. This is $(\mu - \sigma, \mu + \sigma)$, which by the Empirical Rule contains approximately 68\% of the values.
    \item Note $60 = 70 - 2(5)$ and $80 = 70 + 2(5)$. This is $(\mu - 2\sigma, \mu + 2\sigma)$, which contains approximately 95\% of the values.
    \item The interval 65 to 80 is the right half of the 1st standard deviation (34\%) plus the 1st to 2nd standard deviation on the right (13.5\%). Alternatively, $68\% + \frac{95\% - 68\%}{2} = 68\% + 13.5\% = 81.5\%$.
    \item 55 and 85 are 3 standard deviations away from the mean ($\mu \pm 3\sigma$). The proportion outside these limits is approximately $100\% - 99.7\% = 0.3\%$, which is roughly 0.
\end{enumerate}

\item 
\begin{enumerate}
    \item $85 = 100-15$, $115 = 100+15$. $(\mu \pm 1\sigma)$ $\approx 68\%$.
    \item $70 = 100-2(15)$, $130 = 100+2(15)$. $(\mu \pm 2\sigma)$ $\approx 95\%$.
    \item $85$ to $100$ is 34\%. $100$ to $130$ is 47.5\%. $34\% + 47.5\% = 81.5\%$.
    \item 55 and 145 are $\pm 3\sigma$. The fraction outside is approximately 0 (or 0.3\%).
\end{enumerate}

\item 
\begin{enumerate}
    \item $37 = 45-8$, $53 = 45+8$. $(\mu \pm 1\sigma)$ $\approx 68\%$.
    \item $29 = 45-2(8)$, $61 = 45+2(8)$. $(\mu \pm 2\sigma)$ $\approx 95\%$.
    \item $37$ to $45$ is 34\%. $45$ to $61$ is 47.5\%. $34\% + 47.5\% = 81.5\%$.
    \item 21 and 69 are $\pm 3\sigma$. The fraction outside is approximately 0.
\end{enumerate}

% Answers 4-6
\item 
\begin{enumerate}
    \item $340 = 300 + 40$ (1 standard deviation above). By Empirical Rule, the upper tail above $+1\sigma$ is $(100\% - 68\%)/2 = 16\%$.
    \item $(220, 380)$ is $\mu \pm 2\sigma$, so approximately 95\%.
    \item 480 seconds is $\frac{480 - 300}{40} = 4.5$ standard deviations above the mean. This is highly unlikely (probability $\approx 0$).
\end{enumerate}

\item 
\begin{enumerate}
    \item $56 = 50 + 6$. Area above $+1\sigma$ is 16\%.
    \item $(38, 62)$ is $\mu \pm 2\sigma$, so approximately 95\%.
    \item 70 pounds is $\frac{70 - 50}{6} = 3.33$ standard deviations above the mean. Unlikely.
\end{enumerate}

\item 
\begin{enumerate}
    \item $850 = 800 + 50$. Area above $+1\sigma$ is 16\%.
    \item $(700, 900)$ is $\mu \pm 2\sigma$, so approximately 95\%.
    \item 1000 hours is $\frac{1000 - 800}{50} = 4$ standard deviations above the mean. Unlikely.

% Answers 7-12
\item 
$\sum_{i=1}^{n} (y_i - c) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} y_i - \sum_{i=1}^{n} c$. Since $c$ is summed $n$ times, $\sum_{i=1}^{n} c = nc$. Thus, $\sum y_i - nc$.

\item 
$\sum_{i=1}^{n} (ay_i + b) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} ay_i + \sum_{i=1}^{n} b = a\sum_{i=1}^{n} y_i + nb$.

\item 
$\sum_{i=1}^{n} (y_i - \bar{y})^2 = \sum (y_i^2 - 2y_i\bar{y} + \bar{y}^2) = \sum y_i^2 - 2\bar{y}\sum y_i + n\bar{y}^2$. 
Note $n\bar{y} = \sum y_i$. Substitute to get $\sum y_i^2 - 2\bar{y}(n\bar{y}) + n\bar{y}^2 = \sum y_i^2 - n\bar{y}^2$. 
Alternatively, working from the right: $\sum y_i(y_i - \bar{y}) = \sum y_i^2 - \bar{y}\sum y_i = \sum y_i^2 - n\bar{y}^2$. Both sides are equal.

\item 
$\sum_{i=1}^{n} (x_i - \bar{x}) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i - \sum_{i=1}^{n} \bar{x} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i - n\bar{x}$. Since $\bar{x} = \frac{1}{n}\sum x_i$, then $n\bar{x} = \sum x_i$. Thus, $\sum x_i - \sum x_i = 0$.

\item 
$\sum_{i=1}^{n} (y_i - \mu) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} y_i - n\mu$. We know $\sum_{i=1}^{n} y_i = n\bar{y}$. So, $n\bar{y} - n\mu = n(\bar{y} - \mu)$.

\item 
From question 10, $\sum_{i=1}^{n} (y_i - \bar{y}) = 0$. Multiplying by $\frac{1}{n}$ yields $\frac{1}{n}(0) = 0$.

% Answers 31-32
\item 
Let $S_p$ = Spam marked positive (spam), $L_p$ = Legitimate marked positive. 
$P(S_p) = 0.98$, $P(L_p) = 0.02$.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item $P(\text{Both spam}) = 0.98 \times 0.02 = 0.0196$.
    \item $P(S_p) \times P(L_{not\_p}) = 0.98 \times (1 - 0.02) = 0.98 \times 0.98 = 0.9604$.
\end{enumerate}

\item 
Let $I_p$ = Infected tests positive, $H_p$ = Healthy tests positive (false pos).
$P(I_p) = 0.90$, $P(H_p) = 0.05$.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item $P(\text{Both pos}) = 0.90 \times 0.05 = 0.045$.
    \item $P(\text{Wrong}) = P(I_{neg}) \times P(H_p) = (1 - 0.90) \times 0.05 = 0.10 \times 0.05 = 0.005$.
\end{enumerate}
